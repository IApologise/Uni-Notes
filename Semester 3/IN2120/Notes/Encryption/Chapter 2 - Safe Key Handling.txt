For a system to be secure, the keys should:
- Be significant in size
- Implement strong and secure algorithms
- Correctly integrated into software and hardware
- Not be misused and revealed

It is also recommended to create multiple private keys,
such that if one is compromised, everything isn't. Only
a small part of your data may be. Keys are also switched
somewhat often. Its frequency is called a Crypto Period.

The Crypto Period does split into two: Security Period,
and Processing Period. The first is when a data is to
be encrypted and/or digital signature is generated. The
second is decryption and/or signature validation. Even

though Security Period is the most important, the Crypto
Period is decided by the one that lasts the longest. This
means that you may still be using a private key when it
technically has expired and is now considered under risk
of getting hacked with a decent certainty.

The size of the cypher in bits that is acceptable today is
112. However, it is gonna be discontinued after 2030 because
computers are improving and brute forcing gets faster. Today,
128, 192, and 256 are the standards and safe bets.

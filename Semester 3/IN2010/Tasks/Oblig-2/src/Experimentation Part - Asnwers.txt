It seems like my implementation of an insertion sort is better at sorting than my implementation of a quicksort for sizes less than approximately 250, after that quicksort overtakes all the other ones that I had (bubble sort and merge sort). So basically, insertion sort is the best out of those 4 for small lists, and quicksort is best for larger ones.

Also, bubble sort performed the worst. The amount of swaps bubble sort and insertion sort make is exactly the same, but insertion sort makes way less comparisons. Bubble sort makes n^2 comparisons whilst insertion sort makes n^2 / 2. The swaps they make is about 3/4 * n^2.

When it comes to O(n * log(n)) algorithms such as merge sort and quicksort they definitely perform better on larger lists. Quicksort performs about 50% more comparisons than merge sort, but twice less swaps. Swaps is what bottlenecks the performance more than the comparisons, but not by too much. Still, that makes quicksort 30% faster than merge sort.

Overall, both merge sort and quicksort are still faster than O(n^2) algorithms such as bubble sort and insertion sort. At first the difference was negligible, but once we hit a 1_000 size, the quicksort finished in 0.04 seconds and merge sort in 0.06 seconds, whilst insertion sort finished in 0.162 seconds and bubble sort in 0.37 seconds. Those two slower ones did not finish the 10_000 mark since I got tired of waiting about 3 minutes or so, but the O(n * log(n)) bunch did. Merge sort finished in 0.68 seconds, and quicksort in 0.52 seconds. I did not have the patience to wait for more than 30 seconds for 100_000 mark.

The GPU:

CUDA (also known as SP) - Compute unified device architecture.
Purpose: Perform simple and specific tasks.
Variations: FMAs exist in two forms, Scientific & Integer or Floating Point.
Note: Many CUDAs make up a single SM.
Architecture: Has about 410,000 transistors
- 50,000 used on FMA operations (Fused Multiply and Add) which are most common and perform operation A * B + C per cycle (32 bit limit).
Others are used to/for:
- Accommodate negative numbers.
- Bit Shifting.
- Bit Masking.
- Collecting and Queuing instructions.
- Accumulating and Outputting results.

SFU - Special Function Unit.
Purpose: Perform more specialized and complicated calculations such as division, square roots and trigonometry.
Note: SFU is a part of an SM.

SM - Streaming Multiprocessor.
Purpose: Perform a bunch of tasks and control CUDA cores, possesses L1 Cache.
Note: Many SMs and more make up a single GPU where SMs perform calculations.
SMs themselves are made up of a single bunch of Scientific CUDAs, Integer/Floating Point CUDAs, and a single SFU.

GPU (Inside) - General Processing Unit.
Purpose: Perform more, but specific tasks and at a slightly a slower rate than the CPU. Still much faster, just less frequent and versatile than CPU.
Architecture:
- A bunch of SM cores which make up a single GPC (Graphics Processing Cluster) and a controller of type (I assume).
- Gigathread Engine: A controller of a GPU of sorts. It makes sure that the SMs are busy and data is spred evenly across. It utilizes techniques for pipelining its tens of thousands of cores by itself.
- Two L2 Caches
- Single PCIe interface: A huge and a quick cycle data transfer bus (x16 slot) that connects to other devices (aka high bandwidth connector).
- GMC: Graphic Memory Controller which connects to RAMs and VRAMs and controls the data transfer flow. It gets the data from these connections to the SMs.
- NVLink: A connector like GMC between other GPUs allowing you to stack em.

GPU (Outside):
- Display connectors such as HDMI, VGA, DVI and DP.
- 16x PCIe Pins which connect to the motherboard.
- 12 Volt power supply connector followed by a bunch of regulators (VRMs) which convert the power into a stable 1.1 Volt and a bunch of Watts output.
- A cooler of sorts, mostly via turbines but water coolers are also popular.
- A bunch of Graphics Memory Chips (GMCs) which access memory from RAMs of type VRAM. VRAMs are the specialized type of RAM made for GPUs.

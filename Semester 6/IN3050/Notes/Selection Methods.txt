Parent Selection:

1. Roulette Based Selection:
   - Probability to survive based on score
   - Allows more control over selection with an integrated pressure
   - Takes all competitors into account

  To calculate the odds of a roulette based selection you take the
  total score and divide the candidate's score by the total, that is
  its chance to become a parent.



2. Rank Based Selection:
   - Score determines your rank which determines probability of survival
   - Also allows lots of control pressure via probability of selecting worse
     answers randomly
   - This punishes small differences severely, and big differences much less
   - Also takes all competitors into account (except for parallells)

   To calculate the odds of a rank based selection you take the total
   score where if there are n candidates the total score becomes a sum
   so n(n + 1)/2 where the first place has a score of n and last place
   has a score of 1, so odds of a candidate becoming a parent where s
   is the candidate's score is s / (n(n + 1)/2) or 2s / n(n + 1).



3. Tournament Based Selection:
   - Taking a single or few winners from the tournament
   - Allows to control pressure via tournament size and competitor selection
   - Used in case global evaluation is hard

   To calculate the odds in tournament based selection you divide
   the candidates into groups. Within those groups the candidates
   are compared by their fitness score and ranked best to worst.
   When choosing a parent, the best one is chosen from each group.



Elitism (Optional Implementation):
- Always keep some of the best parents, the rest may be offspring
- Reason: Make sure that we do not lose best candidates

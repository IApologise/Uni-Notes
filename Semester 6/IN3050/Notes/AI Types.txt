1. Evolutionary Algorithm:
This is just like the survival of the fittest. Create a bunch
of random stuff and watch them compete over some limiting factor.
Eventually the best survive and the rest die, thus only good are left.



2. Supervised Learning:
Assume that you have categories and have data that needs to be
categorized, this is what supervised learning does. It tries
to categorize data in clumps as much as it can. So it is a classifier.



3. Unsupervised Learning:
This one attempts to split the data into x amount labels
such that it makes sense, so it also kind of categorizes the
clumps, but in its own way without knowing if they are actually
correct in practice, just an estimation.

Assume you want to split a set of natural numbers into two, you
might split them into odd and even, primes and not primes, etc.

5. Reinforcement Learning:
This is a type of learning where an AI is learning as it is acting.
So imagine driving, and as you drive you make mistakes and learn from
those mistakes so that you move towards perfecting your driving skills.

The AI does something similar, it has an initial state which is the
starting environment and observations, and uses it to produce an action.
After it acts the environment picks up on it and gives the next state.
This goes on until we stop or some goal is achieved where at the end we
have a list of states called a single episode or epoch where state is
the environment and AI's actions, so a replay of a simulation / scenario.

To adjust the AI's policy we add a supervisor to the environment, so
the environment also rewards the AI's actions (or punishes or ignores),
the agent then looks at those rewards and punishments and tells AI to
use these actions more often in similar situations or not to. Think of it
as also a part of AI self-improvement algorithm.

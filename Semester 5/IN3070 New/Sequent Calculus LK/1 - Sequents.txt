As we know to prove the validity, a lack there of, and everything
in between, we can use sequent calculus LK where we got sequents
(logical consequences), and branches (splits like in a tree).

A sequent is a single logical consequence such as Γ ⊢ ∆.
But, in this course we use ⇒ instead of ⊢ for some reason.

Branches is whenever a sequent splits into two sequents later.
To prove the validity (tautology), all branches must be closed
by an axiom rule, to prove satisfiability at least one should.

To show falsifiability we must fail in closing one of the axioms
or prove validity of a negated form. For a contradiction, we must
either fail to close all the branches, or prove validatity of the
negated form. So it is similar to falsifiability.

Note that the sequent can be closed by an axiom rule only
if all the assumptions on the left hand side Γ are interpreted
as ⊤ (as it should be), which results in at least one of the
of the formulas in ∆ to also be ⊤ (again, as it should be).

If we want to show a countermodel (invalidity) then we must
show an interpretation I where I(A) = ⊤ for all formulae A
that are in Γ, and that I(B) = ⊥ for all formulae B in ∆.

Technically, using sequent calculus LK we show provability,
but the system is sound (I guess) so whenever we prove something
we also validate it. We should show validity and falsifiability
through a concrete example interpretation or example model. for
example: Take p, p → q ⊢ q. An interpretation that validates it
would be I ⊢ p, and I ⊢ p → q. All that is left is to show one.

--- Introduction ---
Intiutionistic Logic is similar to that of Modal Logic. Instead of Kripke Frames,
we have Intuitionistic Frames F_J which are built the same way as Kripke Frames.
The only difference is that F_J is a specific type of Kripke Frame, an S4 meaning
that it posesses an accessibility relation R being both transitive and relfexive.



--- Interpretation ---
Note that when we interpret in intuitionistic logic, meaning I_J is equal to
(F_J, {I_C(w)}_(for each w in W of F_J)) where I_C is just a normal interpretation
where ALL functions and predicates are defined, and ALL constants and propositional
variables are given a value. It is also called constructive interpretation.

Also, remember that any interpretation can't have domain D where |D| = 0
(e.g. domain D can't have no elements). Remember that I = (D, i) where i is
what defines everything and what assigns values.



--- Forcing Relation ---
Remember that forcing relation denoted as ⊩ is the same as ⊨ except that it is
local for some world w, whereas ⊨ is like for every world w. Anyways, the AND and
OR rules are the same as in Modal Logic. NOT and IMPLIES are different, they require
the inclusion of accessible worlds v from w, world w is where it takes place btw.

So, for w to force -A, denoted as (w ⊩ -A), all the worlds v accessible from w must
revoke (not force) A, denoted as (∀v(v ⊮ A) where (w, v) is in R). So, w ⊩ -A if
any of the accessible worlds v from w do not have v ⊩ A, if at least one v does
have v ⊩ A, then w can't ⊩ -A, so w ⊮ -A.

For implication we do something similar (since not is included). To be more precise,
for w to force (A -> B), all the worlds v accesible from w, whenever v forces A
(v ⊩ A), then v must also force B (v ⊩ B). So if v ⊩ A but v ⊮ B, then w doesn't
force (A -> B) denoted as w ⊮ A -> B.

Unlike in Modal Logic, we don't have boxes [] and diamonds <>. They are now
quantifiers, [] is like a universal quantifier, and <> is an existential one.
So, w ⊩ ∃xA if w ⊩ A for some substitution [x\d] where d is some constant from
our world's evaluation or its domain.

Similarly for w ⊩ ∀xA, but we look at all the accessible worlds, not just the
current one, so if v ⊩ A[x\d] where d is evaluated or in world v's domain, v is
in W, and (w, v) is in R. Note that if the world w or v do not have d evaluated,
then the substitution is ignored I guess and the world is not taken into account.



--- Satisfiability & Validity ---
To show that some formula F is satisfiable we must show that for some interpretation
I of intuitionistic model J, this model I_J validates F, denoted as I_J ⊨ F. To
show that the interpretation I_J validates F, I_J(w, F) = ⊤ is the requirement that
must be satisfied for each world w in W_J. So I_J must show that F is True in all
its worlds w. If that is the case, then F is satisfiable (globally and such).

To show validity, all Kripke Frames J must show satisfiability of F under all the
interpretations I of J. If none Kripke models I_J can show satisfiability, then
that is how we show unsatisfiability. Similar process for showing invalidity.

Remember, J in this context is some Kripke Frame, I is each world's interpretation
of that Kripke Frame J which makes it a model denoted as I_J. Hope it clears it up.
So, whenever we say for every I_J, we mean for every combination of I and J, and
for some I_J, we mean for any combination of I and J.

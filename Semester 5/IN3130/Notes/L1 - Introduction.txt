Our formal models of computation (i.e. programming languages) are made to be simple, but not too simple to allow
us to make anything, i.e. give great freedom. This is done by balancing the ease of use with the ease to reason.
These languages are Turing Complete, such as C, Pseudocode, Lambda calculus, and piet.

After that we get a reminder of alphabets, words, and languages that we learned in IN2080. DFA is mentioned,
and so are TMs, and their respective langauges, but PDAs are skipped. For a reminder on the hierarchy check out
the "L1 - Language Hierarchy.png". Also, in this course we ignore the rejecting state, and instead each undefined
behaviour is automatically mapped to a rejecting state, just like with DFAs, except that in Sipser's book the
TMs there required an explicit mapping. In addition to that, we also assume that the tape has no end at both its
sides, and not just the right side which requires some extra consideration for when the pointer is at the edge
and wants to move past it.

I'd like to quickly point out 2 new operations that I am not sure I've included in IN2080. First is the "◦"
symbol which means concatenation, for example: "1" ◦ "3" = "13". When concatenating DFAs what we do is we
concatenate the inputs they accept, same goes for Languages. Second is the "^+" operator which is identical to
the "^*", aka. the Kleene-Star, but without the empty string.
